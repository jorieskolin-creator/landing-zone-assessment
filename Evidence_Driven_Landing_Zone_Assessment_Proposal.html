01 · The service concept

More than a checklist: a structured path from evidence to action.

The assessment combines senior landing-zone facilitation with a governed analysis layer. The Engine does not replace expert judgment or customer decision authority. It improves the quality, consistency and traceability of the material used for those decisions.

iExpert facilitation

Understand the real platform operating model

Workshops capture both formal design and the silent knowledge that explains how identity, networking, security and application teams actually consume the cloud foundation.

iiLanding Zone Engine

Create a disciplined assessment baseline

The Engine structures platform inventory, documents and workshop attestations, challenges unsupported findings, exposes contradictions and missing evidence, and creates a traceable Summary for discussion.

iiiCustomer outcome

Move from findings to agreed change

The final output is not an automated list of demands, nor a recommendation to buy an accelerator. It is a customer-validated baseline and an agreed set of priorities, decisions, owners and next steps.

02 · What to prepare and how information is handled

Customers are prepared before the workshop days begin.

Before the assessment workshops, the customer is guided on what the assessment is, how the Landing Zone Engine is used, what kind of information is useful, and how privacy and controlled processing are taken into account. This helps the workshops focus on insight and decisions rather than basic orientation.

Useful information

What is helpful to have available

Examples include the tenant or organization construct, management-group / OU / folder diagrams, identity baseline, network design, policy or SCP catalogues, logging architecture, IaC or accelerator repositories, exception registers, and vending or account-factory process descriptions — material already created in normal platform work.

Workshop preparation

How the customer prepares

The customer identifies platform, identity, networking, security and application stakeholders, clarifies cloud and tenant scope, gathers the most useful documents and exports, and understands what kinds of examples, exceptions and silent knowledge will make the workshops more productive.

Safety & privacy

How the Landing Zone Engine protects customer information

The Engine is designed so that raw customer material is not sent to the LLM. Sensitive information is removed deterministically already during the input and sanitization phase before any AI-assisted analysis is allowed. Only the minimum sanitized and structured content needed for the assessment is passed forward.

Assessment information is temporary by design. It is used only for the active session, is not intended to become a persistent external knowledge store, and can be deleted during the session on request. Remaining temporary assessment data is deleted when the session ends.

  • No raw material to the LLMOriginal customer files and live inventory remain outside the AI reasoning boundary.
  • Deterministic sanitization firstSecrets, account numbers and sensitive values are removed before AI-assisted analysis begins.
  • Session-only processingAssessment data is temporary and used only for the active assessment session.
  • Deletion by designTemporary assessment data can be deleted on request and is removed when the session ends.
03 · Why this approach is different

The assessment creates one shared, evidence-grounded point of reference.

Traditional landing-zone reviews often leave information fragmented across architecture slides, interview notes, accelerator checklists and individual interpretations. This model creates one controlled Summary that becomes the Master Data for the final discussion. The Landing Zone Engine Summary is the basis for discussion — not a mandatory verdict. It helps experts and stakeholders discuss what is confirmed, what is uncertain, what matters, and what deserves action from the same evidence-grounded starting point.

01

Better clarity

Configured control, documented intent, stakeholder assertion, planned activity, contradiction and missing evidence remain distinguishable.

02

Better confidence

Uncertainty is made visible instead of being hidden inside a polished “aligned to CAF / Control Tower” narrative.

03

Better use of time

Experts spend less time manually stitching together inventory and notes, and more time validating, interpreting and prioritizing.

04

Better decisions

The assessment ends in explicit customer choices, not only a maturity classification or an accelerator recommendation.

Landing-zone truth model

Platform configuration, documents and workshops are not interchangeable.

Landing-zone truth lives first in the control plane: hierarchy, identity bindings, policy attachments, shared networks and logging destinations. Documents explain intent. Workshops explain ownership, exceptions and whether the platform is actually used. An interview can explain a control-plane fact; it cannot replace one.

Class 1 · Platform

Control-plane evidence

Strongest for “is it so now?”

Live or exported inventory of tenants, management groups or OUs, subscriptions or accounts, policy and SCP assignments, identity roles, hub networks, log archives and vending configuration. Resolves whether a control exists, where it is attached, and whether it is enforced.

Class 2 · Material

Documents and IaC

Strongest for intent and desired state

Landing-zone design, naming standards, exception process, RACI, Bicep / Terraform / accelerator repos. Resolves what the platform team intends to converge to. Drift between repo and tenant is itself a finding.

Class 3 · People

Workshops and interviews

Strongest for operating model

How a new environment is requested, who can create subscriptions, why an exception exists, whether break-glass is rehearsed. May raise or lower confidence on an already evidenced control. Cannot award “embedded” maturity for a missing preventive guardrail.

Scoring rule

Unknown is not zero

Silence is classified, not punished

No evidence after a scoped collector can become tested absence. Silence after only a workshop remains unknown. A silent design area does not receive a red score and does not generate remediation tactics.

CONFIGURED DECLARED UNKNOWN

A design document that says Deny, while the assignment is Audit, is a contradiction — not alignment. Workshops are used to explain the contradiction, not to vote it away.

05 · Step 0 — Prepare & Align

Prepare the customer, the evidence baseline and the workshop logic.

Step 0 happens before the three workshop days. Its purpose is to make sure the customer understands the assessment approach, the role of the Landing Zone Engine, the practical preparation needed, the clouds and tenants in scope, and the boundaries around information handling. If read-only collector access can be granted, inventory is captured here so Day 1 does not start blind.

Purpose

Create a well-prepared starting point

  • Clarify the purpose, scope, clouds, tenants, regions and intended outcomes.
  • Explain how the Landing Zone Engine supports the assessment and why it is used.
  • Set expectations for the workshops, participants and outputs.
  • Highlight the documents, exports and collector access that are helpful to prepare.
  • Explain the Engine’s safety model: deterministic sanitization first, no raw material to the LLM, temporary session-only processing and deletion controls.
Deliverable Prepared Assessment Baseline
What it gives the customer

Better workshops and better outcomes

  • Less time spent explaining the process during the workshops.
  • Better readiness of stakeholders, source material and platform access.
  • Clearer understanding of what the Engine does and does not do.
  • Clear understanding of what information reaches AI, what does not, and how temporary assessment data is deleted.
  • A stronger foundation for efficient evidence collection and later analysis.

Outcome: The customer enters Day 1 informed, aligned and ready.

06 · Day 1 — Understand & Collect

Understand the context and collect the evidence that matters.

Day 1 builds the assessment foundation. The workshops focus on how the landing zone operates in reality — not only what the design says should happen. Platform inventory is ingested alongside documents so the Engine can already show which design areas are evidenced, silent or contradictory.

Purpose

Build a reliable picture of the current state

  • Confirm business objectives, regulatory constraints, scope and assessment expectations.
  • Engage platform, identity, networking, security, application and leadership stakeholders.
  • Review hierarchy, identity, network, policy, logging and vending as they actually exist.
  • Capture contradictions and areas where different teams experience different realities.
  • Identify evidence that may need targeted follow-up on Day 2.
Deliverable Structured Evidence Base
What good evidence looks like

Use material already created in normal work

  • Tenant / organization construct and billing alignment
  • Management group, OU or folder tree and subscription / account placement
  • Identity baseline, PIM or privileged-access design, federation
  • Hub-spoke, Virtual WAN or Shared VPC design and hybrid connectivity
  • Policy, SCP or organization-policy assignments and exemptions
  • Central logging, security account or project, backup and monitoring
  • IaC / accelerator repositories and vending or account-factory process
  • Exception register and operating-model RACI

Principle: do not manufacture documents merely to “pass” the assessment.

07 · Day 2 — Analyze & Clarify

Turn evidence into a credible assessment baseline.

Day 2 is where the Landing Zone Engine creates the structured assessment view. It analyzes the evidence, challenges weak findings, makes uncertainty visible and supports the experts in preparing the customer discussion. The Engine Summary becomes the agenda — not a blank workshop.

Purpose

Analyze, verify and expose uncertainty

  • Close accidental evidence gaps where a collector or document can still be obtained.
  • Analyze capability and anti-patterns across the agreed design areas.
  • Verify whether findings are actually supported by platform objects, documents or attestations.
  • Highlight silent areas, contradictions and missing information.
  • Create GO / WARN / BLOCK Quality Gate status.
  • Calibrate the result with Landing Zone Experts before customer validation.
Deliverable Expert-Calibrated Master Data Summary
Why BLOCK can be valuable

Insufficient evidence is useful information

  • The control may exist but simply not have been collected.
  • The practice may exist informally but not be configured as a guardrail.
  • Stakeholders may disagree about what actually happens.
  • The capability may genuinely not exist.
  • The criterion may not matter for the agreed scope.

BLOCK starts a decision conversation; it does not end the assessment.

Day 2 discipline

Do not let the room vote a silent area green

Targeted questions are asked only where the pack is weak or contradictory: “Inventory shows production subscriptions outside Corp. Is that approved?” “PIM is described; is it enforced on platform roles?” Workshop claims without a platform object remain weak or unknown.

08 · Master Data — Shared Reference Point

The Landing Zone Engine Summary becomes the shared reference point for the assessment.

Master Data for discussion — one structured baseline instead of fragmented opinions. The Summary brings together evidence, findings, contradictions, missing evidence, candidate priorities and action themes so the final workshop can focus on interpretation and decisions rather than reconstructing the assessment.

One controlled Summary · Master Data for the final discussion
  • What is known

    Confirmed platform configuration and evidence-backed findings — including live attachments, IaC that matches the tenant, and attested operating practices that are corroborated.

  • What is uncertain

    Missing evidence, silent design areas, contradictions between document and inventory, and low-confidence workshop-only claims.

  • What needs a decision

    Priority, acceptance, action, deferral, further validation, or an approved exception to a landing-zone standard.

i.

Evidence activates knowledge.

The Engine uses the Landing Zone Knowledge Base and Tactic Playbook to interpret and structure action, but not to invent customer facts. CAF, Control Tower and Cloud Setup guidance never counts as proof that the customer’s platform exists.

ii.

Experts remain responsible for judgment.

The Engine narrows the decision space and surfaces uncertainty; it does not replace contextual interpretation of exemptions, mergers, or regulated boundaries.

iii.

The customer retains decision authority.

The final question is not “what did the Engine demand?” but “what matters enough for us to change?”

09 · Day 3 — Validate & Commit

Validate the findings, prioritize what matters and agree the change.

Day 3 is a decision workshop. The experts do not simply present a report; they use the Summary as the agenda for validating the current state and agreeing where the customer should act. Implementation options remain behind this lock. An accelerator is not selected before the diagnosis is stable.

Possible decisions

Not every gap becomes a remediation item

  • Action approved
  • Further evidence required
  • Risk or gap accepted as an exception
  • Deferred to a later phase
  • No action required
  • Out of scope / not applicable

Principle: landing-zone maturity serves business risk and delivery speed — not the other way around.

Purpose

Turn findings into explicit decisions

  • Confirm, modify or reject major findings.
  • Resolve or consciously retain uncertainty.
  • Prioritize by blast radius, compliance, delivery friction and dependency.
  • Decide what requires action, more evidence, acceptance or deferral.
  • Assign ownership and agree first milestones.
Deliverable Validated Current State + Agreed Change Plan

Part II Proposal — Under Development

Implementation Model · Proposal

From a validated landing-zone assessment to a governed implementation proposal. The proposed Implementation Model is a second, separate Landing Zone Engine capability. It starts only after the assessment Summary has established and locked the current-state findings. Its purpose is to translate agreed findings into practical implementation options, potential responsibilities, dependencies, milestones, technical choices and relevant service capabilities — without allowing accelerator preferences to influence the diagnosis.

Post-assessment capability Validated Summary as input Separate governed pipeline Human approval remains mandatory
Core boundary

First establish what the evidence says. Then decide what matters. Only after that may the Engine propose how an agreed change could be implemented — including Control Tower, Azure Landing Zone accelerators or Google Cloud Setup.

01 · Why a separate implementation function

Keep diagnosis independent from solution design.

The Landing Zone Engine already produces evidence-backed diagnosis, a Planning Decision and a phased WHY / WHAT / HOW roadmap. The proposed next step is not to overload that assessment pipeline. Instead, a separate Create Implementation Plan function would use the validated Summary as its governed source object.

iProtect the diagnosis

No solution-led assessment

Accelerators, Service Portfolio items and technical options must not influence what the Engine concludes about the customer’s current platform.

iiIncrease actionability

Go beyond WHY / WHAT / HOW

Add likely ownership, supporting stakeholders, dependencies, first milestones, success evidence and practical implementation patterns.

iiiSupport buying decisions

Connect evidence to delivery options

Relevant supplier capabilities can be shown only after a validated problem and target outcome establish a genuine reason for them.

02 · Proposed governed flow

The Implementation Model would operate as a second controlled pipeline.

Each stage has a distinct purpose and permission. The implementation proposal can enrich the action path, but it cannot rewrite the validated findings that created the need.

1

Input Gate

Validated Summary

Confirm that the assessment is complete, findings are stable and implementation planning is permitted.

2

Actionability

Eligible Findings

Filter out rejected, accepted, out-of-scope or insufficiently evidenced items. Silent design areas do not activate remediation tactics.

3

Pattern Match

Implementation Model

Match each eligible finding to controlled implementation patterns and approved tactics — hierarchy retrofit, identity hardening, hub-spoke, vending, central logs.

4

Ownership

Responsibility Proposal

Suggest accountable and supporting roles, clearly distinguishing evidence-backed roles from proposals.

5

Options

Technical & Service Choices

Identify plausible technical approaches and relevant Service Portfolio capabilities, including native accelerators as options rather than defaults.

6

Sequence

Dependencies & Milestones

Order the work around irreversible choices first: hierarchy, identity boundary, IP plan, logging destination.

7

Quality Gate

Implementation Proposal

Validate grounding, role claims, technical options, portfolio matches and plan completeness.

Evidence → Diagnosis → Priority → Implementation Pattern → Service Option.
✕  Never Service Option → search for a diagnosis.
03 · What implementation planning would examine

Move from an agreed finding to a practical delivery path.

The Implementation Model should add enough delivery depth to make the proposal usable in a customer decision workshop, while still avoiding premature commitment to a specific accelerator or network pattern.

A

Governance & ownership

Confirm who can decide, own and execute

  • Identify decision authority for tenant, identity, network and policy exceptions.
  • Confirm ownership of the platform landing zone versus application landing zones.
  • Identify decisions requiring customer approval versus routine platform execution.
  • Flag ownership gaps as implementation risks rather than silently assigning them.
B

Baseline & readiness

Validate the starting point before building

  • Verify collector coverage, IaC repository access and exception-process existence.
  • Identify missing data, integrations or privileged-access prerequisites.
  • Separate immediate delivery work from discovery still required.
  • Establish what evidence will later prove the control is inherited and used.
C

Solution & integration design

Define a fit-for-context implementation pattern

  • Present Control Tower, Azure Landing Zone, Google Cloud Setup or custom IaC as options aligned to the customer environment.
  • Reuse existing hubs, identity tenants and logging sinks where they are adequate.
  • Consider access control, IP overlap, hybrid constraints and operational ownership.
  • Use pilots where uncertainty or application-team adoption risk is material.
D

Operating model embedding

Make the change part of normal work

  • A technically complete landing zone is not enough if nobody uses vending or honours exceptions afterwards.
  • Define recurring review of exemptions, drift and new-environment requests.
  • Integrate actions into existing platform and security forums.
  • Include enablement where application teams must change how they land workloads.
E

Measurement & acceptance

Define what successful implementation means

  • Each proposal should contain measurable evidence that the intended outcome has actually been achieved.
  • Examples: time to vend a subscription, share of production subscriptions under the landing-zone parent, policy Deny coverage, log-archive completeness.
  • Track realized risk reduction separately from activity completion.
F

Scale & continuous improvement

Design for repeatability after the first rollout

  • Show how a successful retrofit becomes a repeatable platform rather than a one-time project.
  • Standardize reusable controls, vending templates and exception handling.
  • Plan periodic reassessment against the original landing-zone findings.
04 · Suggested delivery rhythm

Implementation can progress from foundation to embedded operation.

The exact timing should be customer-specific. The model can nevertheless structure work into logical horizons so irreversible choices are addressed before broader rollout.

Foundation

Clarify and prepare

Confirm ownership, hierarchy, identity boundary, IP plan, logging destination, scope and initial measures.

Pilot

Prove the pattern

Implement in a bounded landing zone, validate vending and inheritance, and surface hidden dependencies.

Rollout

Scale the capability

Move remaining subscriptions or accounts, extend guardrails, and retire snowflake paths.

Operate & improve

Make it continuous

Embed exception review, drift management, measurement and periodic reassessment.

07 · Types of proposals

Not every recommendation should be presented as mandatory remediation.

The Implementation Model should visibly distinguish why an option is being proposed. This prevents a useful sales capability from turning into an indiscriminate accelerator catalogue.

Required / Risk-driven

Needed to address a material issue

Used when blast radius, obligation, identity boundary or a confirmed critical gap makes action necessary.

Recommended / Value-driven

Evidence suggests meaningful benefit

Used when the change is not mandatory but has a strong evidence-based case for speed or consistency.

Enabling

Unlocks other agreed actions

Used when one foundational capability — hierarchy, identity or logging — enables several downstream improvements.

Opportunity / Optional

Adjacent value potential

Clearly identified as an optional opportunity rather than a remediation requirement.

08 · Deterministic Implementation Quality Gate

The proposal should earn permission to be shown.

A separate quality gate would prevent fluent but weak implementation advice from being presented as decision-ready.

IMPLEMENTATION GO

Every material action maps to a validated finding, valid pattern and evidence-compatible target outcome. Ownership and technology claims are properly qualified, dependencies are coherent, and service matches are genuine.

IMPLEMENTATION WARN

The proposal is usable, but some ownership, technical choices, prerequisites or dependencies require customer confirmation or additional discovery before commitment.

IMPLEMENTATION BLOCK

An action lacks a valid finding, depends on rejected evidence, introduces unsupported customer facts, or appears to be driven by an accelerator or service rather than an established need.
